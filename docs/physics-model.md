# Pouring model — working note

October 7, 2026. Engineering calibration, not a validated latte solver.

The selected extension retains the conservative Float32 surface, adds transported velocity and a compressible depth-averaged pressure response. A zero-divergence projection would remove the outward source that exposes the foam, so this pass uses a weakly compressible layer. The layer displacement field supplies a pressure gradient; momentum and displacement travel with the flow. Damping represents exchange with the unsimulated bulk. Foam-dependent viscosity represents one ideal wet microfoam, without a yield threshold or arbitrary locking. The previous source is archived in `surface-baseline.ts.txt`; it is not a second production engine.

The circular canvas has a 40 mm radius. The shared jet boundary converts normalized input/ledger quantities to meters, seconds and milliliters. One ledger unit is 12 ml; the full pitcher is 150 ml. This pass uses a prepared canvas at a fixed surface level; it does not simulate cup filling. The milk entering the bulk is recorded as a reservoir quantity. No overflow or in-flight queue is claimed.

Fields: foam is scaled surface milk-equivalent amount per UV area. Its integral divided by 0.045 and multiplied by 12 gives milliliters. Velocity is UV/s, converted to m/s at the jet boundary; layer displacement is an effective compressible surface-layer perturbation in UV units. Color is a separate saturating function of foam, never volume or gas fraction. Deposit, bulk mixing, off-cup quantity and surface foam sunk into the bulk are tracked separately. The force footprint is normalized; incoming impulse is emitted mass times modeled jet velocity. Some vertical momentum becomes outward surface motion through an empirical coupling. No pointer force exists without emitted quantity. Kernel truncation for an impact inside the rim is assigned to the bulk reservoir; impacts outside the cup are counted as off-cup milk.

The jet uses a calibrated exit speed and derives its area from Q / speed, then accelerates under gravity. The desired landing point determines a compatible spout location using the ballistic flight time. Rendering and physics share that clearance, direction and speed. Starts/stops are immediate at the simulation boundary: no residual in-flight milk. Surface retention and impact mixing are empirical smooth functions of jet energy and clearance, with no binary eraser.

Transport uses conservative finite-volume foam fluxes, minmod slope reconstruction and a donor outflow limiter. Each face exchanges equal and opposite amounts. Only pairs of in-cup cells exchange material. Semi-Lagrangian velocity transport, foam-dependent viscosity and a pressure gradient supply connected motion. Boundary velocity is tangent to the circular wall's two-cell band; the grid still approximates a circle. A symplectic pressure update computes continuity from the new velocity; the earlier experimental explicit-Euler update was unstable and is not retained. Momentum damping is gradual, not a stop-time freeze. Numerical mass error and intentional sinks are reported independently. Thin structures still experience numerical diffusion; higher texture contrast cannot recover them.

Input uses a declared 33.3 ms buffer plus fixed-tick/display phase, and only packets received by the current tick deadline. Late timestamps are clamped to the received-time buffer and consumed cursor; late packets cannot edit already consumed history. Scripted trajectories are first sampled at 120 Hz. Recordings preserve event and receipt timestamps, cancellations, settings, recipe and a fresh initial state. Focus loss and stalls over 150 ms cancel pending wet input and record skipped clock intervals; playback applies those intervals without fluid steps or catch-up milk. Recipe changes, refill, fresh cup and finish end recording so its initial recipe and canvas remain reproducible. Real-phone comfort, human reproduction and quantitative reference-video matching remain separate gates.

Calibration uses density 1000 kg/m³, exit downward speed `0.20 + 0.35 sqrt(Q / 12 ml/s)` m/s, and radius from `Q / speed`. Clearance is `2 + 78 height²` mm. Horizontal entrainment is 35% of contact velocity, bounded to ±0.08 m/s; 6% couples into the surface. Vertical-to-radial coupling is `0.025 × (0.6 + 0.8 drift) × visibleFraction`, not a physical impact solution. Visible partition is a smooth empirical energy function documented in `jet.ts`; sinking is volume-weighted and continuous. These numbers are engineering choices, not measured barista fits.

The effective layer is 12 mm deep, with a reduced wave speed of 0.16 UV/s, velocity damping 2.8/s and layer damping 4/s. Viscosity is `4e-6 × (1 + 0.6 min(4, foam)) × (1 + spread)` UV²/s. Velocity is bounded to 0.6 UV/s and layer displacement to ±0.04 UV. Stability substeps bound the wave/advection Courant estimate to 0.45 and diffusion estimate to 0.2. Pressure smoothing uses 1.2e-5 UV²/s. The fixed production grid is 160² at 60 Hz; display quality changes pixel ratio only. These safety bounds are approximations and should be monitored during tuning.

Measured response and honest visual limits are in `physics-response-results.json` and `physics-verification.md`. Pools and neighboring displacement improve, but the small rhythmic fixture still becomes a scalloped column rather than separated striations; the old heart gesture now produces an oval with a thin finishing stroke. Fine-band formation and barista-quality shape control remain unresolved. This model omits aeration, gas fraction, realistic non-Newtonian rheology, cup tilt, bulk circulation, filling/overflow and in-flight milk. Its foam-dependent viscosity is a limited material-response test, not a reconstruction of experimental milk foam.
